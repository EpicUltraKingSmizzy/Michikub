# Michikub design questions (v0.1.0)

These are the non-coding questions that came up while building the first version. Each one has the answer I picked for this build and a one-line reason. Change any answer you like and the code will follow.

Most numbers live in a small set of constants: `BotTuning` (lib/engine/bot.dart), `LayoutTuning` (lib/engine/board.dart), `TableTuning` (lib/ui/game_screen.dart) and `HouseRules` (lib/engine/rules.dart).

## A. Rules and scoring

1. **How is a round scored?** Each loser scores minus the value of the tiles left in their rack (number value, jokers 30), and the winner scores plus the sum of those. *Why:* it's the standard scoring, and everyone already knows it.
2. **What happens when the draw pile runs out?** Play goes on. Passing with an empty pile draws nothing. When every player passes in a row with the pile empty, the round ends, the lowest rack wins, and each loser's penalty is the difference between their rack and the winner's. *Why:* this is the classic rule, and it guarantees that a round always ends.
3. **Is there a target score that ends the whole game?** No. Rounds continue with a running total until you leave. *Why:* game nights vary in length, and a target can be a house rule later.
4. **Who starts?** *(Changed, your decision.)* In Practice (you against robots) you start round 1. In games between people (Pass-and-play and online), the host's game engine picks a random human player to start round 1, so it isn't always the host or player 1. Robot seats at an online table are never picked to start. In every mode the starting player then moves to the next seat each round (clockwise in turn order). A short "*Name* starts" banner shows on the table for about 2 s at the start of each round (`TableTuning.starterBannerMs`). In Pass-and-play it appears after the "Pass the phone" cover is dismissed, so it doesn't sit on top of the cover's text. *Why:* you chose this. A random first player is fair between friends, and rotating keeps it fair over several rounds.
5. **Which way do runs go?** Runs must ascend from left to right, for example 4 5 6. *Why:* a joker's value then comes from its position alone, so it's never ambiguous.
6. **Is a joker's value shown on the tile?** No. Its value comes from where it sits: its position in a run, or the number in a group. *Why:* it keeps the mascot art clean. A small value hint could come later if people get confused.
7. **Can a set be all jokers (for example 3 jokers with 3 or 4 jokers in play)?** No. Every set needs at least one numbered tile. *Why:* otherwise the set has no defined value.
8. **How does a set like `5 joker joker` count?** It counts as the higher-scoring valid reading: the run 5 6 7 (18) beats the group 5 5 5 (15). *Why:* this matters for reaching the first-meld minimum and favours the player.
9. **What does the first meld allow?** It may use only tiles from your own rack, it must reach the minimum (30 by default), and you can't touch the table that turn. Once you have melded, you can rearrange freely on later turns. With the first-meld rule set to Off, there's no minimum and you can rearrange from turn 1. *Why:* these are the classic rules, and Off is the simplest house variant.
10. **Can you take a joker back off the table?** *(Changed in v0.1.4, then v0.1.5.)* You can reuse a table joker after your first meld, but it never goes into your rack (see #64). There are two ways, and the result is the same:
    - **The long way:** move the joker (drag it, or select it and tap) to a new spot or onto another set, then put the tile it stood for into the gap it left. That rejoins the set.
    - **The swap shortcut** *(your design, v0.1.5)*: select one tile, from your rack or elsewhere on the table (tap it, or long-press a single tile), then tap any tile of a set that holds a joker, or drag the tile onto the joker. If the tile is a compatible replacement for the joker's spot:
      - **A run:** the tile takes the joker's position and the joker moves to an end of the run, the high end if both work (7-8-J-10 + 9 gives 7-8-9-10-J; 11-J-13 + 12 gives J-11-12-13). If the run already spans 1-13, the joker is set down alone in the nearest empty space.
      - **A group of fewer than 4:** the tile is added and the joker sits at the group's edge (6-J-6 + 6 gives 6-6-6-J).
      - **A group of 4:** the tile takes the joker's spot and the joker is set down alone in the nearest empty space.
      - When the joker is set down alone, it stays selected (gold) so you can tap a set or a spot, or drag it.
      - A tile that isn't a compatible replacement gets the normal attach logic, which flashes red if that doesn't fit either.
      - A tile taken from the table stays on the table, so #64 is fine (a group it leaves closes up, see #65).

    Either way the table must be valid when you end the turn, so the joker has to end up in a valid set. Reset undoes everything. In a group of 3 with a joker, either missing colour can take its place. Bots can use the swap shortcut too, counted as **1 move**: it's one tile from the rack attached to one set, and the joker shifting along inside that set is part of the same attach, not a separate move of a table tile. When the joker ends up alone (a full 1-13 run, or a group of 4), placing it is a second move. Bots also know the long way, in honest moves (see #33): they move the joker to its new place, then attach the rack tile into the gap.
    - The group 6-6-J plus the missing-colour 6 and a 10-11 in the rack takes 3 moves (put down 10-11, move the J onto them, add the 6).
    - The run 7-8-J-10 plus a 9 (and a rack pair the J completes) also takes 3 moves.
    - If the J can join the end of another table set, it takes 2 moves.
    - A joker at either end of a run works the same way.

    *Why:* it matches the bots and the rule in #64, and the classic swap is still one drop away.
11. **Can you draw after putting tiles down?** No. The button says Draw only while you haven't played anything this turn. Press Reset first if you want to draw instead. *Why:* this matches the classic rules (play or draw, not both).
12. **What happens on timeout?** As agreed: a valid board ends the turn normally. Otherwise the board resets, the tiles return to the rack, the player draws as if passing, and a message says "*Name* ran out of time." *Why:* this follows your spec, and the message explains the sudden change.
13. **What does Pass do when the pile is empty?** The button reads "Pass" and draws nothing. *Why:* it makes it clear that nothing will be drawn.

## B. Table, rack and gestures

14. **Where do dropped tiles land when dropped on top of a set?** The tile is inserted into that set. Dropping on the left half of a tile puts it before that tile, and the right half puts it after. *Why:* it lets you fill a gap or extend at the right edge of the board by dragging alone.
15. **When does a dropped tile join a neighbouring set versus push it aside?** It joins when the result could still become a valid run or group ("compatible", so partial sets of 1 or 2 tiles count). Otherwise the neighbour moves 1 space aside. *Why:* you can build new sets tile by tile, and a wrong tile never merges by accident.
16. **Tap a tile, then tap a 1 or 2 tile partial set?** It joins if it's compatible. With a full set (3 or more), the result must be a valid set or the glow flashes red. *Why:* it keeps tap-building consistent with dragging.
17. **Tap a tile, then tap an empty spot on the table?** The tile goes there. Tapping the rack while a tile you played this turn is selected sends it back. *Why:* tapping can do everything dragging does, which is easier on small screens.
18. **How many rows does the table start with, and how zoomed in is it?** The table keeps 5 rows for the layout rules, but the view starts zoomed in: only 3 rows tall and about 16–21 columns wide (out of 27), so tiles start large. *(Changed in v0.1.3; before, all 5 rows and 27 columns were shown from the start.)* The view zooms out smoothly as play spreads: height and width separately, keeping 2 empty columns beside the outermost sets on each side, up to every row and all 27 columns. *(Changed in v0.1.6: the columns in view are now centred on the board's middle, where end-of-turn tidying gathers the sets (#72), instead of starting at the left edge; before, 3 spare columns were kept on the right only.)* Rows zoom out only when a set actually sits below the rows in view (after Add row, or when a set is placed automatically). *(Changed in v0.1.6: before, a tile in the third row zoomed the view out to keep an empty row below it, so that tile suddenly looked smaller than in rows 1 and 2.)* Rows still grow when something needs room (the never-blocked rule, or Add row). The view zooms back in when the bottom rows empty, and empty rows above the top set are removed at the end of each turn (#74). The zoom never changes during a drag, and tiles glide to their new size. The numbers are `minViewRows`, `minViewCols` (in `TableGeo`) and `spareCols` (in `TableTuning`). *Why:* you found the tiles too small; zooming only when needed keeps them as big as possible.
19. **Should the table shrink when the rack slides up, or should the rack cover the table?** The table shrinks (zooms out) so no row ever hides behind the rack. *Why:* hidden rows would let tiles disappear behind the rack. The trade-off is smaller table tiles while you hold more than 10.
20. **What does the rack do past 20 tiles?** It stays at 2 rows, which share the tiles evenly, and the tiles get narrower. *Why:* a third row would squeeze the table too much.
21. **Should invalid sets be marked during your turn?** Yes: a red outline on sets that aren't valid yet, plus a gold shine when a placement makes a valid set. It can be turned off in Options under Tile highlighting. *Why:* you see problems before you press End turn.
22. **What does Reset do?** *(Changed in v0.1.3: it replaces Undo.)* Reset puts every tile back where it was when your turn began, both the table and your rack order. It does not restart the turn timer. It's greyed out until you've changed something, and the separate "Reset this turn" entry is gone from the menu. *Why:* you asked for one clear way back, and keeping the timer running stops it being used to buy time.
23. **Can you drag or long-press table sets before your first meld?** No. The tiles flash red and a message explains why. *Why:* the move would be refused at End turn anyway.
24. **Do other players watch your moves live?** Yes. Every change you make is shown on the other phones as you make it, with tiles flying from your face-down indicator. *Why:* it's more social, and the host needs your latest board to judge a timeout.
25. **Where do opponents sit?** With 2 players the opponent sits at the top. With 3 they sit left and right, and with 4 they sit left, top and right, in turn order going clockwise from you. *Why:* it matches the mockup and keeps the side columns balanced.
26. **Orientation?** Landscape only. *Why:* the 27-space rows need the width.
27. **Where is the draw pile?** In the top-right corner, with the tile count. *Why:* the right column is taken by the right-hand seat's indicator tiles, and the corner keeps draws visible.
28. **Where do Add row and the sort buttons sit?** *(Changed in v0.1.3; icon fixed in v0.1.5.)* In a column left of the rack, top down: Add row (Make space), shown as the up-and-down arrow symbol drawn much larger so it fills most of the 46 px circle (no text label), then 789 (sort for runs), then 777 (sort for groups). The sort buttons show only 789 and 777, in the same gold as the other buttons' text. Reset and Menu sit above the Draw button on the right. *Why:* this follows your layout notes, and it keeps every rack tool next to the rack.

## C. Computer players (Practice, and bots in online lobbies)

29. **How does bot difficulty work?** *(Changed: your move model, replacing the tile cap.)* A **move** is one action on the table, and each counts as 1: a tile or set from the rack to the board (alone or attached to a set); a tile or set already on the board moved to attach to another or relocated; splitting a set; or taking a tile out of a set. A **play** is a series of moves that takes at least one tile from the bot's rack onto the board and ends with a valid board. Bots plan plays as whole units, and the board is valid after each one. **Move caps per turn:** Easy 6, Medium 12, Hard unlimited. A play whose moves would go over what's left can't be started; the bot tries smaller plays that fit. **Overlooking:** Easy overlooks each play it finds with 40% chance, and Medium with 15%. The decision is made once per play per turn, so a play it overlooked doesn't come back on a re-roll. Overlooking never takes a bot below a **floor** of min(plays available, N) plays, where N is 1 for Easy and 2 for Medium: if it can play, Easy makes at least 1 play, and Medium at least 2 (or 1 if only 1 exists). Hard overlooks nothing and makes every play it finds until none are left. Plays are found again after each one, since the board changes, and the bot picks the best remaining play: most rack tiles, then fewest moves, then most points. All the numbers are `moveCap`, `missChance` and `minPlays` in `BotTuning`. Pacing and the timer rules are unchanged. *Why:* this is your model. *Decided by me:* "available" counts the plays found as the bot goes (so it's min(plays it could actually make, N)). Plays that need more moves than are left don't count as available.
30. **How fast do bots play?** A bot decides its whole turn instantly, with no thinking delay, then carries it out one action at a time. Each action shows on the table and is followed by a pause: 0.5 s for a single tile (a rack tile extending a set, or a tile moved on the table), 1 s for a split or a whole set moved on the table, and 2 s for a new set put down from the rack (all its tiles fly in together). **Start-of-turn delay (new in v0.1.3):** at the start of every turn a bot does nothing for 1 s (`BotTuning.turnStartMs`) before any move or draw, and that second counts against its turn timer. The turn ends after the last pause. Before this, a bot that drew did so at once, which looked as if your Draw made both of you draw. The constants are `turnStartMs`, `singleTileMs`, `splitOrSetMoveMs` and `newSetMs` in `BotTuning`. *Why:* this follows your pacing spec. Today's bots never split or move sets, so the 1 s pause is ready for smarter bots.
31. **What if the timer runs out while a bot is still playing?** The normal timeout rule applies, exactly as for people. If the table is valid, the turn ends with whatever was played, with no draw (it draws only if nothing was played). If the table is invalid, it resets to the start of the turn, the attempted tiles go back to the bot's rack, and it draws as if passing. The bot's remaining actions are cancelled on the spot: every pending action first checks that its turn is still running, so nothing extra lands on the table afterwards. *Why:* this is your rule, and it treats bots the same as humans.
32. **What may a bot do with under 5 seconds left?** Bots plan their turn as *plays*: groups of moves that each finish with a valid table (see #29). With under 5 s left and a valid table, a bot only starts a play whose every move keeps the table valid. Otherwise it ends its turn right away instead of risking a reset. The 5 s is `BotTuning.safeZoneMs`. *Why:* this is your rule, and plays that end valid make the check simple and reliable. It's covered by unit tests in `test/bot_timer_test.dart`.
33. **What can bots do on the table?** *(Changed with the move model.)* Bots find six kinds of play: (1) a new set from the rack (1 move); (2) a rack tile added to the end of a table set (1 move); (3) two rack tiles put down together, plus a spare tile moved over from a table set that stays valid without it, for example taking the black 4 off black 1 2 3 4 to make red 4, blue 4, black 4 (2 moves); (4) a table run split so a rack tile can start its second half, for example 4 5 6 7 8 plus a second 6 becomes 4 5 6 | 6 7 8 (a split plus an attach, 2 moves). (5) a joker reclaim (2 or 3 moves, see #10; added in v0.1.5 in this honest form); (6) the joker swap shortcut (1 move, plus 1 to place a joker that was set down alone, see #10). The engine also supports moving whole sets and removing a tile from a set (each 1 move, 1 s or 0.5 s pacing), ready for smarter plays later. Bigger rearrangements, such as several sets reshuffled at once, aren't planned yet. *Why:* these plays are legal, easy to follow on screen, and use every kind of move your model counts.
34. **How does the first meld fit the move model?** *(Changed.)* Before its first meld a bot may only put down sets from its rack, so the first meld is one play: rack sets that together reach the minimum, with each set 1 move. Overlooking can drop sets from what it sees, but the floor means Easy and Medium still make their first meld when they can. If it would need more sets than the move cap allows, it keeps the highest-scoring sets that still reach the minimum within the cap; if it can't, it draws. *Why:* the floor says a bot that can play makes at least 1 play.
35. **Bot names and avatars?** There's a final roster of 6 robots (Pixel, Sparky, Lumi, Clank, Tank and Duke were cut from the earlier 12) in `lib/app/bot_roster.dart`. Each has an id, name, tagline and avatar picture in `assets/bots/robot_<name>.png`, scaled down from the 512 px art to 256 px:
    - Bolt: "Plays fast, thinks later."
    - Cogsworth: "Sorts every rack, judges yours."
    - Rusty: "Been playing since tiles were wood."
    - Nova: "Dreams of the perfect 13-run."
    - Echo: "Watches what you play, then copies it."
    - Gizmo: "Always rearranging something."

    Practice setup picks N different robots at random as soon as it opens. It shows each robot's picture, name and tagline next to its difficulty choice, and Shuffle robots picks again. Difficulty is separate, so any robot can be Easy, Medium or Hard, and the level is added to the name in game, for example "Nova (Medium)". Long-press a robot's avatar at the table to see its tagline. Bots added to an online lobby take a robot nobody at the table is using, and the lobby shows their level and tagline. The personalities are flavour only for now: every robot of the same difficulty plays the same way. *Why:* you approved this roster. Making the taglines change how each robot plays (Echo copying you, Gizmo always rearranging) is a possible later step.
36. **Can bots fill empty seats online?** Yes, the host can add Easy, Medium or Hard bots in the lobby. *Why:* a 2-person online game can become a 4-player table.

## D. Online play

37. **What format is the room code?** 6 characters with no look-alikes (no 0/O or 1/I/L). *Why:* it's easy to read out loud.
38. **What if the host leaves?** The game ends for everyone with "The host closed the game." There's no host migration. *Why:* the host's phone holds the game, so moving it is a bigger feature.
39. **What if a guest drops?** Their seat shows "(offline)", their turns pass automatically after 5 seconds, and joining again with the same code and name gets the seat back. *Why:* one bad signal shouldn't stop the game.
40. **Can someone join a game in progress?** Only to take back their own seat. New players wait for the next game. *Why:* there are no extra tiles to deal mid-round.
41. **How do you invite friends?** *(Changed in v0.1.5.)* With the room code: Share or Copy invite in the host lobby (see #59-60), or friends type the code. The copy-and-paste "Invite by message" link (no matchmaking service) was removed. *Why:* your call; one way to invite is simpler.
42. **Who edits the house rules?** The host only. Guests see a summary in the lobby and in the game menu. *Why:* it gives one source of truth.
43. **Do Test tools affect online games?** No, only Practice and Pass-and-play. *Why:* so nobody gets a joker handed to them in a real game.
44. **Is an unfinished game saved if the app closes?** Not in v0.1. *Why:* it's planned for the polish step, and the engine state is already plain data that can be saved.

## E. Look, sound and wording

45. **Sounds?** None in v0.1. Vibration (light taps on pick-up and drop) is on by default and can be switched off in Options. *Why:* sound needs real assets, and silent play suits game nights.
46. **Avatars?** Everyone uses the same circular frame: a 3 px #c9a24a gold ring with a drop shadow (board A style), plus a soft gold glow on whoever's turn it is. Inside the frame, robots show their picture clipped to a circle, and people show their initial on their seat colour. The frame is drawn by the app, so the art itself has no ring. The same frame appears at the table, in Practice setup and in the online lobby. *Why:* one consistent frame matches the board mockup, and people have nothing to upload or store.
47. **Default name?** "Player", changeable in Options or on the Join screen. *Why:* there's no sign-up.
48. **What does the main button say?** "Draw" when you haven't played, "End turn" once you have, and "Pass" when the pile is empty. On others' turns it shows a greyed "Waiting..." with an hourglass. *(Changed in v0.1.3: it used to say "Draw / End turn".)* Draw ends your turn straight away, and only your rack gets the draw animation. *Why:* the button always says exactly what will happen.
49. **What does the Pass-and-play cover screen say?** *(Changed in v0.1.5.)* "Pass the phone to *Name*", with no subtitle line, and the button "I'm *Name*, start my turn". Your turn timer starts only when you tap the button. *Why:* nobody loses time while the phone changes hands.
50. **What does the results screen show?** The winner, why the round ended, each player's leftover tiles, the round score and the running total, plus Next round and Home. *Why:* the leftover tiles make the scoring easy to check.
51. **Is there a pause?** Opening the menu in Practice or Pass-and-play pauses the timer and the bots. Online games can't be paused. *Why:* you can't pause other people's phones fairly.
52. **What's the home screen tagline?** "Play with friends. No ads, no subscription." *(Changed in v0.1.3, your wording.)* The logo on the home screen is also bigger (170 px, up from 110). *Why:* it says what makes Michikub different.
53. **Should the joker art be thicker?** The line art was thickened for tile size (a bolder version of your 4-colour drawing). *Why:* the original lines vanish at about 30 px.

## F. Changes from phone testing (v0.1.3)

54. **How does sorting work?** *(New in v0.1.3.)* 789 and 777 first pull out the best valid sets of 3+ tiles in your rack (most tiles, jokers included) and put them top left, each followed by a small gap (40% of a tile). The remaining tiles follow, sorted for runs (colour, then number) or for groups (number, then colour). Ties between sets lean towards runs for 789 and groups for 777. A set is never split across the two rack rows when there's room to keep it together. *Why:* you asked for sets first; the gaps make each set easy to see and drag out.
55. **How do you know it's your turn?** *(New in v0.1.3.)* Your avatar keeps its gold glow, and the rack now glows gold too, with a brighter gold edge. *Why:* the rack is where you're looking.
56. **How big is the face on the app icon?** *(Changed in v0.1.3.)* On Android the whole drawing now fits inside the 66 dp safe circle (its farthest line at 95% of the circle's radius), so the face is about 89% of the safe circle's height and 71% of its width. Before, a default 16% inset from the icon tool shrank it to about two thirds of that. The lines are drawn a little bolder so they hold up at launcher size, and the iPhone icon's face is enlarged too, to 88% of the icon's height. There's a preview of the old and new icons in `icon_preview_v2.png`. *Why:* you found the face too small on your phone.
57. **How are tile numbers sized?** *(Changed in v0.1.3.)* Every number from 1 to 13 uses the same font size (the old one-digit size) and the same baseline. Two-digit numbers are fitted by tightening the space between their digits, not by shrinking them. *Why:* 10–13 looked smaller and sat lower than 1–9.
58. **Is "Michikub" shown on the game table?** *(Changed in v0.1.3.)* No, it's removed from the top-left corner, which now only shows the round number. *Why:* it took space without telling you anything during play.

## G. v0.1.4

    "Come play Michikub with me!" / "Room code: ABCD" / "michikub://join/ABCD". "Copy invite" copies the same text. *Why:* the wording you picked; the code is still readable if the link doesn't open.

Some chat apps don't make custom michikub:// links tappable; then you copy the code. A web (https) link that always works would need a hosted page, so it's left for later.

## H. v0.1.5

61. **What is Player 1 called in Pass-and-play setup?** *(New in v0.1.5.)* It starts as the player name saved in Options, and you can still edit it there. The other players keep their last-used names. *Why:* the phone's owner is usually Player 1.
62. **What happens right after you draw in Pass-and-play?** *(New in v0.1.5.)* Your turn is over at once, but before the pass-the-phone cover appears your rack stays on screen for 2.5 s (`PassPlayTuning.drawRevealMs`), with the drawn tile(s) landing in it with a gold glow. This also happens after a timeout draw. Nothing can be touched during that pause, and the next player's timer doesn't start until they tap their button. *Why:* you see what you drew before handing the phone over.
63. **Can you long-press tiles and then tap where they go?** *(New in v0.1.5.)* Yes. Long-press still grabs a compatible run or group from the rack, or a whole set on the table, to drag. If you let go without moving, the tiles stay selected (gold glow). Then:
    - Tap an empty spot on the table: they go there as a new set (the snap grid and layout rules apply, and it's never refused).
    - Tap a set: they attach if the result is valid, at the end nearer your tap when either end works. If it isn't valid, the selection flashes red and stays selected.
    - Tap the selection again, or the rack background: deselected.

    This works for rack selections any time and for table sets on your turn. *Why:* your request; it's easier than a long drag on a small screen.
64. **Can a tile go back into a rack?** *(New in v0.1.5, your rule.)* Only a tile you put down from your rack this turn. Every move into a rack, other than drawing from the pile, is checked: "was this tile on the table at the start of the turn?" (a snapshot of tile ids taken when the turn begins). If yes, the move is rejected. You'll see a red flash and the tile snaps back, with "Tiles that were on the table at the start of the turn can't go into a rack."
    - This covers dragging, tapping, long-press selections and whole sets dropped on the rack.
    - The game engine checks every preview and End turn, the same for Practice, Pass-and-play, bots, and moves received from online guests. A guest's phone can't sneak one through.
    - Bots never plan such a move, and the bot player checks each step before showing it.
    - Reset isn't a move: it puts back the table as it was at the start of the turn.

    *Why:* table tiles belong to everyone; only the pile feeds racks.
65. **What happens to a group when a tile leaves it?** *(New in v0.1.5, your request.)* The group closes up, keeping its left-most position: taking the middle 7 of 7-7-7-7 leaves 7-7-7, not 7-7 _ 7. This applies to every way of moving a tile: drags, taps, long-press selections, the joker swap and bot moves. Runs don't close up: taking a tile from the middle of a run still splits it, because that's a split. *Why:* a gap in a group is never useful, and closing it saves a move.
66. **Can you copy just the room code?** *(New in v0.1.5.)* Yes: long-press the room code in the host lobby (or the "Room ABCD" title on a guest's lobby). That copies only the code, with a light vibration (if haptics are on) and a short "Code copied" note. Share and Copy invite still send the whole invite. *Why:* sometimes you only want to read out or paste the code.
67. **What if the host backs out of the lobby by accident?** *(New in v0.1.5, your request.)* On the Host a game screen, every way back asks first: the back arrow, the Android back button or gesture, and the iOS swipe (which is turned off there). The dialog reads "Exit the room?" with **No**, the default (tapping outside the dialog also counts as No), and **Yes**. Yes closes the room, tells anyone who joined that "The host closed the game.", disconnects them, and goes back to Home. Starting the game isn't affected. *Why:* one stray swipe shouldn't throw your friends out.
68. **How do typing dialogs cope with the keyboard?** *(Fixed in v0.1.5, from your phone test.)* Dialogs you type into (Save as preset) are built for a landscape phone with the keyboard taking most of the height. Everything scrolls together, the buttons always sit below the field, and the text field keeps its normal height. Save as preset has no title any more (your call): just the "Preset name" field, Cancel and Save, and the keyboard's Done also saves. The Options, Join and Pass-and-play screens already scroll above the keyboard. *Why:* the old dialog squeezed the field until the buttons sat on top of it.
69. **What font is the room code in?** *(New in v0.1.5, your pick.)* Space Grotesk Bold, still gold with wide letter spacing. It's used in three places: the host lobby (36 pt, slightly smaller than the old 40 pt Playfair because Space Grotesk is wider), the code in a guest's "Room ABCD" title (20 pt next to the 22 pt Playfair "Room"), and the Join a game code box (22 pt). Other titles are unchanged. The font's OFL licence is bundled and shown under Options > Open-source licences, and credited in the README. *Why:* clear, distinct letters that are easy to read out loud.

## I. v0.1.6

70. **Do you have to press End turn when your last tile goes down?** *(New in v0.1.6, your rule.)* No. As soon as a player has no tiles left and the table is valid (every set valid, first meld rules met), the round ends and the results show. This is the same for people and bots in every mode. In online games the host's phone decides it from the move it receives, so a guest can't end a round with an invalid table.
71. **What happens when you drop a tile into the middle of a run?** *(New in v0.1.6, your request.)* If the set is valid with the tile where you dropped it, it stays there. If not, but the set is valid with the tile at one end, it goes to that end: a yellow 4 dropped into the middle of 5-6-7-8 makes 4-5-6-7-8, not 5-6-4-7-8. The same goes for several tiles: 1-2-3-4 dropped onto 5-6-7-8 makes 1-2-3-4-5-6-7-8. When both ends work (a joker, say), it goes to the end nearer the drop. A set that isn't valid yet (still being built, like a lone 5 or 9-9) also takes tiles that keep it compatible, so you can build sets by dropping. With no valid placement, the set moves out of the way instead (#75). A tile that fits a group just joins it. Dropping a tile onto a joker it can replace still does the joker swap shortcut (#10). If the end is at the board edge, the set slides over to make room.
72. **Does the table tidy itself between turns?** *(New in v0.1.6, your request.)* Yes, after every played turn (people and bots). In each row the sets move toward the middle of the table, in the same left-to-right order, with exactly 1 empty space between them. The host does this and sends the result, so every phone shows the same table. A turn that ends in a draw leaves the table as it was at the start of the turn (already tidy). Reset is unaffected.
73. **Do rows collapse?** *(New in v0.1.6, your rule.)* At the end of each played turn, in this order:
    1. While more than 3 rows have tiles: a set that is alone in its row moves into an adjacent row that has tiles, at the same columns or 1 column left or right, if it fits with a space on both sides. Same columns are tried first, then 1 left, then 1 right; rows are checked top-down, above before below.
    2. While the rows from the top set to the bottom set span more than 3 rows and one of the rows in between is empty: the rows on the side nearer that empty row move one row inward. For example, with rows 1, 3 and 4 in use, row 1 moves down to row 2.
    3. Compacting (#72).
    4. Empty rows above the top set are removed (#74).
    *Why this order:* sets move between rows based on where you left them, and compacting comes last so every row ends neatly centred.
74. **Does the view zoom back in?** *(New in v0.1.6, your request.)* Yes. When the bottom rows are empty, the view shrinks back (to no fewer than 3 rows). At the end of each turn the empty rows above the top set are removed, so the table starts at the top and the view zooms in. Tiles glide to the new size; nothing changes mid-drag, and dropping a tile into the last visible row still never zooms out (#18).
75. **What if the tile you drop on a set doesn't belong in it?** *(New in v0.1.6, your rule.)* It stays exactly where you dropped it, as its own set, and the set is shunted aside. A blue 4 dropped onto a yellow 5-6-7-8 stays put and the yellow run moves over. The set moves away from the drop point: left if you dropped on its right half, right if on its left half. If that side has no room, it goes the other way. Neighbouring sets are pushed along with 1-space gaps by the usual row rules, and a set only moves to another row if the row is full. This applies to single tiles and multi-tile drags, and to groups as well as runs: a tile that can't join a group doesn't belong in it either, so the same rule keeps things predictable. Joker swaps and valid placements (#71) are tried first.
76. **When does "Save as preset" show?** *(New in v0.1.6, your request.)* Only once the options differ from the defaults (Classic), in Practice, Pass-and-play and Host a game. Change an option back and it hides again. It also stays hidden when the options match a preset you already saved (picking that preset), since there would be nothing new to save. *(Also in v0.1.6: the helper text under Computer players on the Practice screen is gone.)*

## J. v0.1.7

77. **How long is the last room code remembered?** *(New in v0.1.7, your request.)* Until the app is closed. It's kept in memory only, never saved to the phone. Join a room and the next time you open Join, that code is already in the box. Tap the box once and the old code clears so you can type a new one. If you start typing or editing straight away, it doesn't clear again. A code that came in through an invite link always wins over the remembered one and isn't cleared by a tap.
78. **What happened to the "YOUR TURN" text under my name?** *(New in v0.1.7, your request.)* It's gone. Whose turn it is already shows in the gold ring and the End turn and Draw buttons. Opponents keep their turn clock.
79. **What happens when you drop a tile into a run that already has that number?** *(New in v0.1.7, your rule.)* The run splits at that tile, and your tile starts the right-hand part. A yellow 4 dropped into yellow 3-4-5-6 makes 3-4 and 4-5-6, side by side with a space between. Both parts are valid as long as each ends up with 3 or more tiles; if not, you fix it before ending the turn as usual. This only applies to a single tile (not several dragged together), to runs (a group can't hold the same tile twice anyway), and to the exact same colour and number. Tapping a rack tile to auto-place it uses the same split when nothing better fits.
80. **What happens when you finish a group of 4 that has a joker?** *(New in v0.1.7, your request.)* The joker comes out. Adding the missing 4th colour to 4-4-4-J gives 4-4-4-4, and the joker is set down on its own beside it and selected, ready for you to use. This is the same as the joker swap shortcut (#10). As with every joker swap, you need your first meld down first.
81. **When does a joker move inside a run?** *(New in v0.1.7, your rule.)* When moving it makes your drop valid. Yellow 2-3-J plus a yellow 4 makes 2-3-4-J: the 4 takes the joker's place and the joker moves to the end (or the start, if the end has no room in the number range). The order of checks when you drop a tile on a set:
    1. Dropped straight onto a joker it can replace: the joker swap (#10).
    2. Valid where you dropped it (#71).
    3. Valid at one end (#71).
    4. Valid if a joker in the set moves (this rule). The joker can be anywhere in the set.
    5. Duplicate number in a run: split (#79).
    6. A set still being built takes tiles that keep it compatible (#71).
    7. Otherwise the set is shunted aside (#75).
    *Why this order:* placements that keep your tile where you dropped it come first. Moving the joker is a bigger change than going to an end, and a split is bigger still, so they come later. Tapping a rack tile to auto-place it tries a valid end, then the joker move, then the split.
82. **How does the table use the side margins?** *(New in v0.1.7, your request.)* The table no longer keeps empty spare columns at both sides. The view is centred on the sets, and when a set reaches the edge the view slides over before it zooms out. It only zooms out when the sets really need more than the 16 columns that fit. The seat columns on the left and right are also a little narrower, giving the tiles more width. Nothing moves mid-drag.
83. **Which tiles glow white, and when does the glow stop?** *(New in v0.1.7, your request.)* Tiles that other players put on the table since the end of your last turn. That covers new tiles and ones moved in from the rack, not tiles that were only rearranged. The glow shows during other players' turns and at the start of yours. It fades out (in 0.7 seconds) as soon as you play a tile or draw, and stays off for the rest of that turn. Each phone tracks this for its own player, and it resets at each new round. In pass-and-play, each player's glow is tracked separately. On the results screen, the tiles the winner played on their final turn glow, even if the winner is you.
84. **Can I hide the results to look at the table?** *(New in v0.1.7, your request.)* Yes. "See the table" on the results card hides it so you can look at the final table (with the winner's last tiles glowing, #83). A "Show results" button in the bottom right brings it back. Next round and the other buttons are on the results card as before.
85. **Can I arrange my rack during other turns, and leave gaps?** *(New in v0.1.7, your request.)* Yes, both. The rack is a grid of slots, 10 to a row. Drop a tile into any slot: an empty slot takes it, and an occupied one pushes tiles along only as far as the next empty slot. A tile you move or play leaves an empty slot behind. A tile you draw goes after your last tile, or into the last empty slot once the rack has 20 or more slots, so it doesn't keep growing. Sort packs the rack again, keeping each sorted set on one row where it fits. Your arrangement is kept between turns. During other turns you can only move tiles within your rack.
86. **Do I get a tile count like opponents?** *(New in v0.1.7, your request.)* Yes. Your avatar has the same count badge as opponents, showing the tiles in your rack right now. It goes down as you play tiles in your turn, and back up if you reset.
*(Also in v0.1.7: an opponent's drawn tile now appears in their face-down rack only when its flight from the pile lands, not before.)*

## K. v0.1.8 (in progress)

87. **Where do the side seats' face-down tiles start?** *(New in v0.1.8, your request.)* Below the seat's whole name plate, however tall it is. A bot's "Name (Level)" usually takes two lines in the narrow side column, so its tiles start lower than a person's one-line name. The plate is measured with room for the turn clock, so the tiles don't jump when that player's turn starts. If a column of 10 wouldn't fit above the bottom corners (your name and avatar on the left, the buttons on the right), the tiles get a little shorter instead of overlapping anything. The right seat also sits a few pixels lower than before (at least 6 px, more if needed), so its avatar and count badge always clear the pile count ("48 IN PILE"). The top seat is unchanged.
88. **How does a sort mode work?** *(New in v0.1.8, your request.)* Tapping 789 or 777 sorts the rack and selects that mode (the button turns gold with black text). While it's selected, the rack sorts again whenever a tile comes in: a draw, a tile taken back from the table (dragged or tapped), Reset bringing tiles back, or a new round's deal. Playing tiles to the table doesn't re-sort; their slots just go blank. Moving a tile within your rack by hand, on any turn, deselects the mode, so your arrangement is never sorted over. Each player's mode is kept separately (pass-and-play) and lasts until the app closes.
89. **Where does a drawn tile go?** *(New in v0.1.8, your rule.)* In the first empty slot after your last tile, so blanks you left between sets stay where they are. The rack holds two rows of 10. If the slot after your last tile would be past those 20, the tile takes the first blank in the rack instead, and with no blanks the rack grows. (Before, it took the last blank once the rack had 20 slots, which could look random.)
90. **Where is an opponent's turn clock?** *(New in v0.1.8, your request.)* On its own line under their name, for every opponent (people and bots, side and top seats). When turns are timed, that line is always kept, even when it's not their turn, so the face-down tiles start below it and don't move when the clock appears. A long name can no longer push the clock out of view.
91. **What does the turn-start pulse look like?** *(New in v0.1.8, your request.)* A soft warm-white glow flashes up around your rack and fades over about 0.65 seconds when your turn starts. It doesn't block taps.
92. **What do room codes look like now?** *(New in v0.1.8, your request.)* 6 letters, no digits. I, L and O are left out because they look like 1 and 0, leaving 23 letters. The Join box only accepts letters, and links or codes with digits (from older versions) aren't recognised.
*(Also in v0.1.8: on the Practice and Pass-and-play setup screens, Start is in the top right corner.)*
